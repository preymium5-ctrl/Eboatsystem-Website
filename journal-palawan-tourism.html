The Philippines' tourism boom and the case for quieter boats

The Philippines' coastal and island destinations continue to attract millions of visitors, creating significant opportunities for sustainable boating and marine transportation.

According to the Department of Tourism's 2024 overnight traveller statistics: Batangas 4.45 million, Zambales 2.14 million, Cebu 5.08 million, Aklan including Boracay 2.20 million, Negros Occidental 1.72 million, Bohol 1.37 million and Iloilo 1.36 million.

Meanwhile, Palawan welcomed more than 2.1 million tourists in 2025, including 821,504 in Puerto Princesa, 642,385 in El Nido and 336,304 in Coron, according to the Palawan Provincial Tourism Office.

A decade
of growth

Asian Development Bank data show tourist arrivals in Coron and El Nido growing from 76,815 in 2008 to over 900,000 in 2019. In El Nido alone, approximately 600 tourist boats were registered in 2025 (Philippine Information Agency).

Other emerging and established destinations, including Nasugbu, Anilao, Subic, San Antonio, Dumaguete, Siquijor, Guimaras and Dapitan, present further opportunities for sustainable marine tourism.

Pressure
on the water

Growing tourism activity increases demand for island-hopping, diving, snorkelling and recreational boating, placing additional pressure on fragile marine ecosystems.

Traditional diesel and petrol-powered boats contribute to underwater noise, exhaust emissions and the risk of fuel spills in the same lagoons, beaches and coral reefs that attract visitors.

A cleaner and
quieter alternative

Starting in Batangas and Zambales, ESMI aims to introduce modular solar-electric boats and plug-and-play solar-powered boat engines that reduce fuel expenses, maintenance requirements, noise and marine pollution.

By embracing renewable energy, tourism operators can offer cleaner, quieter and more environmentally responsible experiences while protecting the natural resources their businesses depend on.

Growing without
the noise

Quiet, fuel-free boats let operators offer the premium, eco-conscious experience many travellers now look for, while protecting the places their business depends on. That is the opportunity ESMI is building for, starting in Coron.
