Why fuel costs are pushing boat operators to rethink

Across the Philippines, island-hopping, dive and fishing trips still run on diesel and gasoline outboard engines. Boat hulls have slowly moved from wood to fiberglass, but propulsion has barely changed, and fuel remains one of the largest daily costs of running a bangka.

A sharper squeeze
in 2026

In April 2026, a pumpboat operator in Mactan, Cebu, told Rappler that a 10-litre fill which used to cost about ₱500 now cost ₱1,500. On a typical minimum ₱5,000 boat rental, that means roughly 30% of the trip's revenue goes straight to fuel. The jump followed the Philippines declaring a national energy emergency in March 2026, with diesel briefly reaching ₱173 per litre in some areas.

What changes
with the sun

An ESMI boat charges from its own solar roof and from shore power, so the daily fuel bill disappears. Operators still invest in the boat, upfront, financed or through rent-to-own, but the monthly payment can be weighed directly against the fuel they no longer buy.

An electric drive also has far fewer parts to wear out than a combustion engine, which means fewer of the repairs that keep boats off the water.
